Intuition

Before anything can be retrieved, documents have to be prepared, like a library cataloguing new books: read the file, clean it up, cut it into passages, record what each passage is about, and file it so it can be found fast. That is the offline half of RAG. The online half happens per question: find the best passages and hand them to the model.

How you cut the passages matters more than it sounds. Cut a rule away from its exception, or a sentence away from its heading, and the right answer can never be found again.

Mechanism

RAG has an offline path (prepare documents) and an online path (answer a question). Each stage has design choices that affect quality.

Example: why context headers help

A chunk reading "The limit is 5 days per year" is ambiguous. With a prepended header "Employee Handbook › Leave › Bereavement leave (2026)" it embeds and retrieves correctly for "how many bereavement days do I get?", and the model can cite it unambiguously.

The numbers

How many chunks. 10,000 documents of 2,000 words, cut into 400-word chunks with 50 words of overlap, move forward 350 words at a time: about 2,000 ÷ 350 ≈ 6 chunks per document, so about 57,000 chunks in all.

What overlap costs. 50 words of overlap on every 350 new words stores and embeds about 50 ÷ 350 ≈ 14% more text, in exchange for fewer facts cut in half.

What a header costs. A title like "Employee Handbook › Leave › Bereavement leave" adds about 15 tokens to each chunk: 15 × 57,000 ≈ 855,000 extra tokens to embed once, a small price for chunks that make sense on their own.
