This page walks through Lewis et al. (2020), Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks in plain words. Section and table numbers point into the paper. It is the paper that named RAG.

1. The problem

A large language model keeps what it knows inside its weights. The authors list three weaknesses: you cannot easily expand or revise that memory, you cannot see what a prediction was based on, and the model may produce hallucinations (section 1). Earlier work had added a retrieval step to extractive question answering. This paper asks what happens when the generator is itself a sequence-to-sequence model that writes the answer.

On the TriviaQA split that the closed-book T5 models used, RAG-Token scored 66.1 and RAG-Sequence 68.0. In section 4.1 the authors report that RAG still answered 11.8% of NQ questions correctly when the answer was in none of the retrieved passages, which an extractive model cannot do.

For question generation (Jeopardy), 452 pairs of outputs were judged by people. Judges found the RAG output more factual than BART's in 42.7% of pairs and BART's more factual in 7.1% (Table 4, section 4.3).

Section 4.5 swapped the index without retraining. For 82 world leaders, asked with "Who is {position}?", the 2016 index gave 70% correct for 2016 leaders and the 2018 index gave 68% for 2018 leaders. The mismatched pairs scored 12% and 4%. In the dev-set ablation (Table 6), a retriever that was trained scored 43.5 on NQ for RAG-Token, against 37.8 when it was frozen.

4. What it does and does not show

  • Shows: a generator that reads retrieved passages beat a closed-book model of similar scale on NQ, and its answers could be traced to passages. It also shows that a knowledge source can be swapped without retraining.
  • Does not show: that RAG beats every retriever-only system. On the standard TriviaQA split DPR scored higher than both RAG models (Table 1), and on one of the other sets RAG-Token was below DPR.
  • Does not show: anything about company documents, permissions, chunking choices or modern chat models. The index is one Wikipedia dump, cut into 100-word chunks.
  • Failure the authors report: in story generation, the retriever sometimes learned to return the same passages whatever the input, and the generator then ignored them (Appendix H).
  • The hot-swap test used a narrow question template about world leaders, so it shows the idea, not general freshness.
