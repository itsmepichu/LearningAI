Intuition

In the game of telephone, a message passed through ten people arrives garbled, even if each person is careful. Agent chains behave the same way: one worker’s invented fact becomes the next one’s starting point and the final report’s headline. That is error compounding.

Checks between steps break the chain: require evidence for claims, verify the important ones, and cap how many rounds agents may argue before a person decides.

Mechanism

  • Conflicting outputs: two workers report different facts. Resolve with evidence (prefer the claim with a verifiable source), recency, source authority rules, or a targeted verification step; surface unresolved conflicts to the user rather than averaging them away.
  • Coordination overhead: every delegation and result costs tokens for briefs, summaries and synthesis; agents may duplicate each other's searches. Overhead can exceed the useful work on small tasks.
  • Error propagation: one worker's hallucinated fact becomes the coordinator's premise and the final report's headline. Reliability compounds: if each of 6 sequential steps is 95% reliable, the chain is 0.95 × 0.95 × 0.95 × 0.95 × 0.95 × 0.95 ≈ 74% reliable. Verification steps and evidence requirements break the chain.
  • Runaway loops: agents delegate back and forth ("please clarify" ↔ "please clarify"), an evaluator never approves, or a coordinator keeps spawning workers. Use global step, depth and spawn limits, a run-level budget, loop detection on repeated messages, and a maximum number of critique rounds.
Example: a runaway critique loop

An evaluator agent is told "approve only when the report is perfect". It requests changes 14 times; the producer keeps rewriting; $4.20 is spent on one report. Fix: a rubric with concrete pass criteria, a maximum of 2 revision rounds, and after that, ship with the evaluator's remaining notes attached for a human.

The numbers

Steps that are each 95% right.

  • 3 steps: 0.95 × 0.95 × 0.95 ≈ 0.857, so 86% of runs are fully right.
  • 6 steps: about 74%. 10 steps: about 60%.

Adding a verifier that catches 60% of errors. Each step is now right 95% of the time, plus 60% of the other 5%: 95% + 3% = 98%. Over 6 steps: 0.98 multiplied 6 times ≈ 89%, up from 74%.

A runaway critique loop. 14 rounds at 0.30 dollars each is 4.20 dollars for one report; a cap of 2 rounds costs 0.60 dollars.

Code

The program computes how reliability falls over 1 to 10 steps, with and without a verifier, checks the 6-step figure by simulating 20,000 runs, and prices a capped critique loop. Set STEP_OK to 0.99 or CATCH to 0.9 and run it again.
