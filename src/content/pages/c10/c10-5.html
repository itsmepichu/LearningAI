Shard: a subset of the index on one node. Queries fan out to all relevant shards and results are merged (scatter-gather). Sharding scales data size and write throughput.
Replica: a copy of a shard on another node. Replicas scale read throughput and provide availability when a node fails.
Partitioning strategy: how documents map to shards. By tenant (each query hits one or few shards; good isolation; risk of uneven size). By hash of chunk ID (even spread; every query hits every shard). Hybrids: tenants get dedicated shards when large, shared shards when small.
Hot tenant: one tenant with far more data or traffic than others, overloading the shard(s) it lives on: a "noisy neighbour" (15.5).

Capacity planning for an index: memory (vectors + graph + metadata, × replicas, plus headroom for rebuilds, typically 30–50%), query throughput per replica (measured QPS at target latency and recall), write throughput (upserts per second during backfills), and growth rate. Plan to re-shard before shards exceed what a node can rebuild quickly after failure.

Example: a mixed strategy

A SaaS platform has 12,000 tenants; 11,950 have under 100k chunks and share 16 shards (placed by tenant ID hash, so each query touches one shard). The 50 largest tenants get dedicated collections with 2–6 shards and replicas sized to their traffic. A tenant is promoted to dedicated when it passes 5M chunks or 50 QPS.
