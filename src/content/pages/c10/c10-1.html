Intuition

Before building a search system for millions of documents, do the arithmetic on the back of an envelope. Documents become pages, pages become tokens, tokens become chunks, and every chunk becomes a vector that has to live in memory, usually more than once. Each step multiplies, so a small wrong guess early on becomes a big wrong bill at the end.

It is like planning a library: count the books, estimate the shelves, then add room for copies, the catalogue and growth.

Mechanism

Capacity planning starts with a chain of multiplications. Each assumption (pages per document, tokens per page, chunk size, overlap, embedding dimensions, precision, replicas) changes the result several-fold, so measure them on a sample of your real corpus.

Example: the default numbers

10M documents × 6 pages × 500 tokens = 30B tokens. With 512-token chunks and 10% overlap that is ≈ 65M chunks. At 768 dims in float32 that is 200 GB of vectors; int8 brings it to 50 GB, plus ≈ 8 GB of graph links, ×2 replicas ≈ 116 GB of RAM across the cluster. Embedding the ≈ 33B chunk tokens (overlap included) once costs ≈ $670 at the assumed API price, and every embedding-model change repeats that cost (10.7).

The numbers

The default numbers, step by step.

  • Tokens: 10 million documents × 6 pages × 500 tokens = 30 billion tokens.
  • Chunks: each 512-token chunk moves on about 461 tokens (10% overlap), so 30 billion ÷ 461 ≈ 65 million chunks.
  • Vectors: 65 million × 768 numbers × 1 byte (int8) ≈ 50 GB. As float32 it would be 200 GB.
  • Graph links and metadata: about 8 GB and 20 GB more, so about 78 GB for one copy.
  • Two replicas plus 40% headroom for rebuilds: 78 × 2 × 1.4 ≈ 218 GB of memory.
  • Embedding the whole corpus once, counting the overlap, at $0.02 per million tokens: about 33 billion × 0.02 ÷ 1,000,000 ≈ $660.
