Intuition

A service can be fast, available and wrong. Operational dashboards would show green while answers get worse, so AI systems need signals about behaviour: was the task done, was the answer grounded, did retrieval find anything, did tools fail, did agents loop?

Some of the best signals come from what users do rather than what they say: asking the same question again in other words, copying the answer, or opening a ticket afterwards. This implicit feedback is available at once, long before slow outcomes arrive.

Mechanism

Operational health does not tell you whether answers are good. Add signals specific to AI behaviour:

  • Task success: explicit feedback (thumbs, ratings), implicit signals (user copied the answer, did not rephrase, did not open a ticket afterwards), and outcome events (refund processed, ticket resolved).
  • Groundedness / quality samples: run judges (8.2) on a sample of production traffic continuously; track scores by feature, tenant and language.
  • Retrieval failures: zero-result queries, low top-score queries, abstention rate, citation-validation failures.
  • Tool errors: invalid arguments, permission denials, timeouts, per tool.
  • Agent loops and budgets: steps per task distribution, loop-detector triggers, budget exhaustion rate.
  • Escalation rate: hand-offs to humans; both too high (unhelpful) and suddenly too low (overconfident) are signals.
  • Safety signals: guardrail block rate, injection-classifier hits, user reports.
Example: an implicit-feedback signal

"Rephrase within 60 seconds" (user asks essentially the same question again) correlates strongly with unhelpful answers in many assistants. Computing it needs only conversation logs and an embedding similarity check, so it is available immediately, unlike delayed outcomes (17.7).

The numbers

Sampling quality (illustrative). Judging 500 sampled conversations a day out of 200,000 is 0.25% of traffic. If groundedness is around 95%, 500 samples pin it to within about ±2 points, enough to see a drop to 81% on the first day.

Escalation rate. If 4% of 10,000 daily conversations go to a person, 400 a day is normal. A jump to 900 means unhelpful answers; a fall to 50 may mean the assistant has become overconfident.

Code

The program builds simulated sessions from your project’s messages, where an unhelpful answer often leads to the question being asked again in other words, and measures how many of those the "rephrased within 60 seconds" rule finds. Change the similarity threshold and run it again.
