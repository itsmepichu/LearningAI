• Sampling: storing every span for millions of requests is expensive. Head sampling decides at the start (keep 5% at random): cheap but misses rare failures. Tail sampling (in the Collector) decides after the trace completes: keep all errors, slow traces, low quality scores and flagged safety events, plus a random baseline. Keep metrics unsampled.
  • Redaction: prompts and outputs contain personal and confidential data. Options per field: do not record (default for message content in many conventions), record hashed, record redacted (16.5), or record in a separate, more restricted store with shorter retention. Never log secrets or auth tokens.
  • Retention: e.g. metrics 13 months, sampled traces 30 days, full content 7 days in a restricted store, audit logs per legal requirement. Deletion requests must reach telemetry too.
  • Telemetry costs: LLM traces are large (prompts can be tens of KB). Budget storage and ingest; drop high-cardinality attributes from metrics (e.g. never use raw user IDs as metric labels).
  • Watch for missing telemetry: export dropped-span and queue-full counters from SDKs and the Collector, and alert on them. A silent pipeline looks exactly like a healthy, quiet system.
Example: a Collector redaction rule
processors:
  attributes/redact:
    actions:
      - {key: gen_ai.input.messages,  action: delete}          # content not kept in the main trace store
      - {key: gen_ai.output.messages, action: delete}
      - {key: user.email, action: hash}
  tail_sampling:
    policies:
      - {name: errors,  type: status_code, status_code: {status_codes: [ERROR]}}
      - {name: slow,    type: latency,     latency: {threshold_ms: 8000}}
      - {name: baseline, type: probabilistic, probabilistic: {sampling_percentage: 5}}
In practice · HarborHarbor keeps all error and slow traces, a 5% random sample of the rest, strips document bodies, and alerts on dropped spans.
Failure to avoidLogging every prompt forever: a security liability and a cost problem.
