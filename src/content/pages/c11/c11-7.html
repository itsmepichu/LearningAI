• Risk tiers: classify actions. Read-only → automatic. Reversible low-value writes → automatic with audit. Irreversible, financial, external communication, or permission changes → human approval.
  • Approval UX: show the proposed action, its exact parameters, why the agent proposes it, the evidence (trace links), and the consequences. Approvers need to be able to edit or reject with a reason, which becomes feedback data.
  • Escalation: hand the task to a human queue with a summary when the agent is uncertain, hits a limit, sees conflicting data, or the user asks for a person.
  • Step limits and budgets: per-task maximum steps, tokens, dollars and wall-clock time; per-user and per-tenant daily budgets. Enforce in the runtime, not the prompt. When a budget is exhausted, stop and escalate with the work so far.
  • Approve an exact version: an approval applies to one immutable action (target, arguments, content hash). If the agent edits the email or the amount afterwards, it needs a new approval, and cancelling must stop executions that are already queued.
Example: policy as configuration
agent: support-resolver
limits: {max_steps: 12, max_tokens: 60000, max_cost_usd: 0.40, max_wall_clock: 10m}
actions:
  get_order:      {tier: read,  approval: none}
  propose_credit: {tier: write, approval: none,     max_amount_usd: 50}
  propose_credit_over_50: {tier: financial, approval: role:support_lead, timeout: 24h, on_timeout: escalate}
  send_email:     {tier: external, approval: none,  template_only: true}
escalation: {queue: tier2-support, include: [summary, trace_url, proposed_actions]}
