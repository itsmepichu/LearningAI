Intuition

A new GPU replica is not a light switch. Finding a machine, pulling a multi-gigabyte image, loading tens of gigabytes of weights and warming up can take minutes: the cold start. If you only add replicas when they are already busy, the morning rush is over before help arrives.

So scale on signals that move early, such as the queue of waiting requests, keep a few replicas warm, and only call a replica ready once it has actually answered a test request.

  • Packaging: a container image with a pinned engine version, model weights pulled from an internal registry or object storage at start (or baked in for faster starts), configuration (TP size, max model length, memory fraction, quantisation) as versioned config, health and readiness endpoints.
  • Cold start: scheduling a GPU node (minutes if the cluster must scale), pulling the image (GBs), loading weights (tens of GB from storage), allocating KV cache, compiling CUDA graphs. Total can be several minutes, so autoscaling must anticipate demand.
  • Warm-up: send synthetic requests of typical shapes before marking ready, so first real users do not pay compilation and cache-allocation costs.
  • Autoscaling signals: GPU utilisation is a poor signal; use queue depth / waiting requests, KV-cache utilisation, and TTFT against SLO. Keep a minimum warm pool; scale down slowly.
  • Readiness means a real answer: mark a replica ready only after the model and tokenizer are loaded and a small validation inference succeeds, not when the port opens. Scale-to-zero saves money but conflicts with tight first-request latency targets.

The numbers

A ramp (illustrative). Load grows from 2 to 10 replicas’ worth between 8:00 and 9:00, about one more replica every 7.5 minutes.

  • With a 12-minute cold start, a replica requested at 8:15 arrives at 8:27, by which time load has grown by about 12 ÷ 7.5 ≈ 1.6 replicas.
  • If the signal also lags by 5 minutes, you are behind by about 17 ÷ 7.5 ≈ 2.3 replicas through the whole ramp.
  • Keeping 3 extra replicas warm costs 3 × 24 × 2.50 = 180 dollars a day at 2.50 dollars an hour (illustrative).
