Intuition

Renting a car by the hour or buying one: buying wins only if you drive a lot, every day. Self-hosted GPUs cost the same whether busy or idle, while an API charges per token used.

The break-even volume depends on things that are easy to get wrong: the throughput you really get at your latency target, how busy the GPUs are at night, and the engineers who keep them running.

Mechanism

Self-hosting cost is mostly fixed: you pay for GPU-hours whether or not they are busy. API cost is variable: you pay per token. Self-hosting wins only when (a) your volume is high and steady enough to keep GPUs well utilised, (b) a smaller open model meets your quality bar, or (c) data-control requirements force it. Include the full cost: GPUs (with redundancy and headroom), engineers and on-call, networking, storage, observability, idle capacity at night, and the opportunity cost of not using newer hosted models.

Example: reading the default numbers

20B tokens/month at $0.60/M via API = $12,000/month. Self-hosted at 2,500 tokens/s per GPU and 45% average utilisation, one GPU delivers ≈ 2.96B tokens/month, so 7 GPUs ≈ $12,800 plus $15,000 ops ≈ $27,800. The API is cheaper here; self-hosting wins only at several times the volume, higher utilisation, or with a much cheaper model.

The numbers

The default example (illustrative).

  • API: 20 billion tokens × 0.60 dollars per million = 12,000 dollars a month.
  • One GPU at 2,500 tokens a second and 45% average use: 2,500 × 0.45 × 2,592,000 seconds ≈ 2.9 billion tokens a month.
  • 20 ÷ 2.9 ≈ 6.9, so 7 GPUs × 2.50 dollars × 720 hours ≈ 12,600 dollars, plus 15,000 dollars of operations ≈ 27,600 dollars.

Here one GPU costs 1,800 dollars for 2.9 billion tokens, about 0.62 dollars per million, already more than the API before operations, so no volume makes it pay. At 70% use the same GPU costs about 0.40 dollars per million.
