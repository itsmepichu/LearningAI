• Tensor parallelism (TP): split each layer's matrices across GPUs in one node (e.g. TP = 4 or 8 over NVLink). Needed when weights + KV do not fit one GPU; also reduces per-token latency. Communication every layer.
  • Pipeline parallelism (PP): split layers across GPUs or nodes; used for very large models across nodes.
  • Expert parallelism (EP): place MoE experts on different GPUs; tokens are routed over the network to their experts (all-to-all communication).
  • Data parallelism / replicas: run several independent copies behind a load balancer; the main way to scale throughput. Prefix-aware routing sends requests with the same prefix to the same replica to hit its cache.
  • Disaggregated serving: run prefill and decode on separate GPU pools (compute-heavy vs bandwidth-heavy), transferring the KV cache between them. Lets each pool be sized and tuned independently; used in large-scale deployments (e.g. NVIDIA Dynamo, llm-d, and SGLang/vLLM disaggregation features).
  • Redundancy counts units, not GPUs: a model split across 4 GPUs with tensor parallelism is one serving unit, and losing any one GPU stops that unit. Plan redundancy as N+1 complete units.
Example: sizing a 70B deployment

70B in FP8 ≈ 70 GB of weights. On 80 GB GPUs, TP = 2 leaves ~70 GB for KV across the pair (≈ 210k tokens at 320 KiB/token in BF16); TP = 4 leaves ~215 GB and lowers latency per token. For 600 concurrent 4k-token conversations (≈ 2.4M tokens of KV ≈ 790 GB), you need roughly 4 replicas of TP = 4, before headroom.

In practice · HarborHarbor runs its 70B model as two TP=4 units; losing one GPU takes one unit out while the other keeps serving.
