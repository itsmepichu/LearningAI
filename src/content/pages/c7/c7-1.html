Intuition

Using a model through a provider’s API is like taking a taxi: you start in minutes, pay per trip and never think about maintenance. Running an open model yourself is like buying a car: more work up front and more responsibility, but cheaper if you drive a lot, and nobody else sees where you go.

Whichever you pick, do not choose a model by its fame or a public leaderboard. Test a few on a hundred or so of your own real cases and pick the cheapest one that is good enough, the way you would test-drive cars on the roads you actually use.

Selecting a model with evidence. Public leaderboards measure general skills on public data, which may be contaminated and rarely matches your task. Instead, build a small task-specific evaluation set (50–200 real examples with expected outcomes) and run 3–5 candidate models through it, measuring quality, latency and cost together. Choose the cheapest model that meets the acceptance bar, not the best-scoring model overall.

Your first evaluation set (start of chapter 8)

For a ticket-classification feature: export 150 real tickets, have two people label category and urgency, keep the 140 they agree on, and write a 20-line script that runs a model over them and reports accuracy per category plus median latency and cost. This is your baseline, your model-selection tool and your first regression test. Chapter 8 grows it into a full evaluation practice.

The numbers

When self-hosting pays (illustrative prices). A hosted call with 1,500 tokens in and 200 out, at $1 and $4 per million tokens, costs $0.0023. One rented GPU at $2.50 an hour costs about $2.50 × 730 hours = $1,825 a month.

  • Break-even: $1,825 ÷ $0.0023 ≈ 790,000 requests a month, if that one GPU can serve them all.
  • Below that, the hosted API is cheaper; above it, self-hosting can be, before counting the people who run it.

How sure is an evaluation? A model that gets 138 of 150 cases right scores 92%, but with only 150 cases the true figure could be anywhere from about 87% to 96%. Two models scoring 91% and 92% on 150 cases are a tie.
