• Multi-region: run in two or more regions. Active-active: both serve traffic, routed by latency or residency; more complex data consistency. Active-passive: standby region takes over on failure; simpler, slower failover.
  • Data residency: EU users' data and prompts may need to stay in EU regions and EU-hosted model endpoints; this constrains routing and failover targets.
  • Disaster recovery: define RPO (recovery point objective: how much data you may lose, e.g. 5 minutes) and RTO (recovery time objective: how fast you must recover, e.g. 30 minutes) per component. Vector indexes can be rebuilt from stored chunks and embeddings, but rebuilding takes hours at scale, so replicate or snapshot them.
  • Provider outages: hosted model APIs have outages and regional incidents. Mitigate with a second provider or self-hosted fallback (evaluated for quality and prompt compatibility), regional endpoints, circuit breakers, and the degradation ladder. Test failover regularly; untested failover usually fails.
  • Restore, don't just back up: test restores on a schedule. Replicas copy corruption and accidental deletes instantly, so they are not backups. Rehearse failing back to the primary region too, including replaying queued writes exactly once.

Quarterly, the team blocks Provider A at the gateway in staging, then in production for 10% of traffic. Findings from the first run: the fallback model ignored the JSON schema in 4% of responses (fixed by enabling constrained decoding), and fallback quota was too small for full traffic (raised in the provider contract). Without the exercise, both would have surfaced during a real outage.
