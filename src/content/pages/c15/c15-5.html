Intuition

A model gateway is the one front door to every model the company uses. Every request passes it, so it is the natural place to choose the right model, hold the credentials, count usage, enforce budgets and switch to a fallback.

It is also where fairness is enforced. Without limits, one customer’s runaway script fills the queue and everyone waits. With per-tenant quotas and fair queuing, the tenant who caused the burst is the one who waits.

Mechanism

A model gateway is a central service between applications and all model backends (hosted providers and self-hosted pools). It provides one API, credentials management, routing, quotas, retries and fallbacks, caching, usage metering, logging and policy enforcement. Without one, every team reimplements these and nobody can see total spend.

  • Routing: by task (cheap model for classification, strong model for complex reasoning), by tenant tier, by load (least-loaded replica, prefix affinity), by region/data residency, by health (skip a degraded provider).
  • Tenant quotas: per-tenant limits on requests/min, tokens/min, concurrent requests, and spend/day, enforced with token buckets.
  • Fairness: when capacity is short, give each tenant its fair share instead of first-come-first-served, e.g. weighted fair queuing by tier.
  • Noisy-neighbour protection: one tenant's spike (a runaway agent, a bulk script) must not raise latency for everyone; quotas plus per-tenant queues plus admission control contain it.
Example: gateway routing rules
routes:
  - match: {task: classify}                      to: pool/small-8b-selfhosted
  - match: {task: answer, tenant_tier: free}     to: provider-b/mid-model
  - match: {task: answer}                        to: provider-a/large-model
    fallback: [provider-b/large-model, pool/self-hosted-70b]
  - match: {region: eu}                          constrain: {provider_region: eu}
quotas:
  default:    {rpm: 600, tpm: 400000, concurrent: 40, usd_per_day: 200}
  enterprise: {rpm: 6000, tpm: 4000000, concurrent: 400, usd_per_day: 5000}

The numbers

The noisy-neighbour example. Capacity is 100 units a second. Tenants B to E send 15 each (60 in total) and tenant A normally sends 10.

  • A bursts to 6 times normal, 60: total demand 120 against 100, so 20 a second pile up.
  • First come first served: everyone’s requests sit in the same growing queue, so B to E wait as long as A.
  • Fair queuing: B to E get the 60 they need; A gets the remaining 40 of its 60, so only A’s queue grows.

Code

The program lets tenant A send 1, 3 and 6 times its normal load into shared capacity and compares each tenant’s waiting time under first come first served and fair queuing. Add a burst of 10 and run it again.
