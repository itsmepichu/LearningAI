• Backpressure: propagate "slow down" upstream (bounded queues, 429 with Retry-After) instead of accepting work you cannot finish in time.
  • Load shedding: when overloaded, reject some requests early and cheaply (lowest priority first: batch, then free tier) so the rest succeed. Reject based on predicted wait vs deadline; a request that will time out anyway should not consume GPU time.
  • Circuit breaker: wrap each dependency. After an error-rate threshold, the breaker opens and calls fail fast (or go to fallback) for a cool-down period; then it goes half-open and lets a few trial calls through; success closes it, failure re-opens it. Prevents piling threads onto a dying provider.
  • Graceful degradation: a ladder of reduced service levels: smaller model, fewer retrieved chunks, no reranker, shorter max output, disable non-essential features (suggested follow-ups), cached answers only, and finally a clear "temporarily unavailable, here's search" message.
Example

During a viral launch, queue wait reaches 12 s against a 10 s deadline. The gateway sheds free-tier batch requests (L4 for them), moves free-tier chat to L2, and keeps paid tiers at L0. Overall success rate stays above 99% for paid users, and the incident report shows exactly which levels were active and when.
