Intuition

The cheapest model per call is not always the cheapest way to get the job done. Failed attempts still cost money, and a failure that a person must fix costs far more than the call.

Cost per successful task counts everything, including retries and people, and divides by the tasks that really succeeded. It turns "which model is cheaper?" into "which design solves problems for less?"

Mechanism

Cost per request is misleading: retries, failed agent runs, abandoned conversations and escalations to humans all cost money without delivering value. Cost per successful task = total cost (model tokens, embeddings, retrieval infrastructure, tools, GPU time, human review) ÷ number of tasks that met the success definition (8.1). It ties engineering choices to business value and makes tradeoffs visible: a cheaper model with a lower success rate can cost more per success.

  • Capacity forecasting: project the 15.1 chain forward with growth assumptions per tenant and feature; add known events (launches, onboarding of a large customer, seasonal peaks); compare to measured capacity per replica (15.10); order GPUs or raise provider quotas with lead time.
  • Budget enforcement: hard limits per request (max tokens), per task (agent budgets, 11.7), per user and tenant (daily spend in the gateway), and per feature (monthly). Alert at 50/80/100%; at the limit, degrade (smaller model) or block, depending on the tenant's contract.
Example: attributing cost

Monthly AI spend rises 40% with flat traffic. Cost attribution by feature and tenant (17.5) shows one tenant's integration calling the summarise endpoint on every page view. A per-tenant daily budget and a cache for identical documents cut that tenant's cost by 85%.

The numbers

Two options for 100,000 tasks (illustrative), with 1.50 dollars when a person handles a failure.

  • Small model: 0.004 dollars a try, 70% success. Model 400 dollars; 30,000 failures × 1.50 = 45,000 dollars. Total 45,400.
  • Large model: 0.012 dollars, 88% success. Model 1,200 dollars; 12,000 × 1.50 = 18,000. Total 19,200.

The model that costs three times as much per call costs less than half as much overall. Trying the small model first and retrying failures on the large one costs about 18,800.
