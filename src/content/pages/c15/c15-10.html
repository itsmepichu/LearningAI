Three kinds of numbers are routinely confused in capacity discussions. Label every figure with which kind it is.

KindSourceExampleTrust
Measured performanceBenchmarks and load tests you ran, under stated conditions"One replica sustains 2,400 output tok/s at p95 ITL 45 ms with our traffic mix (load test 2026-10-28)"High for those conditions; does not transfer to other prompt mixes or hardware
Projected capacityCalculation from measured numbers + assumptions"12 replicas should support 30k output tok/s at peak"As good as its assumptions; state them
Actual production usageTelemetry from real traffic"Peak last Monday: 18.2k output tok/s; p95 ITL 52 ms"Ground truth for the past; the input to the next forecast
Example: a projection that failed (illustrative scenario)

A vendor benchmark said 5,000 tok/s per GPU; the team projected 20 GPUs would cover launch. Their own load test with real prompt lengths (median 3k input tokens, long tail to 30k) measured 1,700 tok/s at their latency SLO. Production at launch needed 52 GPUs. The fix is procedural: capacity plans cite measured numbers with test conditions, and launch reviews compare them with the first week's actual usage.

In practice · HarborHarbor's launch review states: "measured 60 RPS on staging with p95 1.8 s for this workload; projected 100 RPS with two more replicas, not yet validated".
Failure to avoidDescribing a simulation or extrapolation as a system that served millions of users.
