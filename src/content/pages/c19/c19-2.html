Intuition

A vision-language model is a language model with eyes bolted on. A vision encoder looks at the picture and writes down what it sees as vectors, a small adapter translates those vectors into the language model’s "alphabet", and the language model reads them as if they were words.

It can read text in images, describe scenes, compare pictures and answer questions about charts. It is also confidently wrong in new ways: it misreads small digits, miscounts objects and mixes up left and right.

Mechanism

Three parts.

  • Vision encoder: usually a vision transformer trained with images and captions (CLIP-style), turning patches into vectors.
  • Projector: a small network, often one or two layers, that maps those vectors to the language model’s hidden size. Training usually starts by teaching only the projector to align images with captions, then fine-tunes more of the model on image–instruction pairs.
  • Language model: reads image tokens and text tokens in one sequence, so attention connects words to image regions.

Cost and speed. Image tokens are input tokens: they lengthen prefill and so the time to first token (14.2), and they take KV-cache memory. Sending ten images at full resolution when one cropped image would do is the most common waste.

Example: choosing a resolution

A returns team checks photos of damaged parcels. At low detail (about 85 tokens per photo) the model spots crushed boxes but misses torn labels; at high detail (about 1,000 tokens) it catches both. They send low detail first and re-ask at high detail only when the model reports "unsure", which cuts image tokens by about 70%.

The numbers

One 448 × 448 image with 14-pixel patches: (448 ÷ 14) × (448 ÷ 14) = 32 × 32 = 1,024 patches; merged 2 × 2, 256 tokens.

  • With a 40-token question: 256 + 40 = 296 tokens of prefill.
  • 16 such images: 16 × 256 + 40 = 4,136 tokens. At 10,000 prefill tokens a second, about 0.4 seconds before the first word, compared with 0.03 seconds for one image.
  • KV cache (14.1) at 128 KiB per token: 4,136 × 128 KiB ≈ 0.5 GB for one request.
