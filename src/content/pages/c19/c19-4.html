Intuition

A voice assistant is three systems in a relay: one listens and writes down what was said (speech-to-text), one thinks and answers in text, and one reads the answer aloud (text-to-speech). Some newer models do all three in one, listening and speaking directly.

In conversation, timing matters as much as accuracy. People notice a pause of more than about a second, and they interrupt. The system has to know when the caller has stopped talking and stop speaking when the caller starts.

Mechanism

Measuring recognition. Word error rate counts the substituted, deleted and inserted words against a human transcript, divided by the words actually spoken. Report it per language, accent and audio channel: phone audio, Tamil speech mixed with English words, and names, numbers and product codes are where errors concentrate.

Cascaded or speech-to-speech. A cascade is easier to debug, log and control: every step has text you can check, redact and evaluate. Speech-to-speech models sound more natural and respond faster, but tool calls, logging and evaluation need extra care, and transcripts may still be needed for audit.

Numbers by voice

"Fifteen" and "fifty" sound alike on a bad line. For amounts, dates and account numbers, read them back and ask the caller to confirm before acting.

The numbers

Word error rate. The caller said "cancel my booking to Chennai" (5 words). The transcript reads "cancel the booking Chennai": one substitution (my → the) and one deletion (to).

  • WER = (1 + 1) ÷ 5 = 40%. A WER of 5% means about 1 word in 20 is wrong.

A latency budget (illustrative). End-of-speech detection 300 ms + recognition 250 ms + the model’s first token 450 ms + first audio 200 ms = 1,200 ms before the caller hears anything. Cutting the turn detection to 200 ms saves 100 ms but interrupts slow speakers more often.
