Intuition

A language model only reads tokens. To show it a photo, the photo is cut into small squares, like tiles on a floor, and each tile becomes one vector: an image patch. Sound is cut into short slices of time in the same way, each slice an audio frame.

So a picture is not "one input"; it is hundreds or thousands of tokens. That is why images are expensive, why models shrink large images first, and why a long recording can fill a context window faster than a long document.

Mechanism

Every modality goes through the same three steps: cut the input into pieces, encode each piece into a vector, and place the vectors in the sequence the language model reads, next to the text tokens.

Resolution decides cost. Models resize large images to a maximum side, and many split big images into tiles plus a small overview, so fine print stays readable. Doubling the width and height of an image roughly quadruples its patches. Providers document how they count image tokens; read it before estimating cost.

Merging. Many models merge each 2 × 2 group of neighbouring patch vectors into one token after encoding, cutting the count four times with little loss.

Example: a support screenshot

A user sends a 1,920 × 1,080 screenshot of an error. Shrunk to 1,024 wide, it becomes about 780 tokens, more than the user’s whole written question. Cropping the screenshot to the dialog box before sending it cuts that to about 150 tokens and often improves the answer, because the model is not distracted by the rest of the screen.

The numbers

Counting patches for a 1,024 × 768 image with 14-pixel patches:

  • Across: 1,024 ÷ 14 ≈ 74 patches (rounding up). Down: 768 ÷ 14 ≈ 55.
  • Patches: 74 × 55 = 4,070. Merged four into one: about 1,018 tokens.
  • At an illustrative 3 dollars per million input tokens, 1,000 such images cost about 1,018 × 1,000 × 3 ÷ 1,000,000 ≈ 3 dollars.

Audio. At 50 frames a second, a 10-minute call is 600 × 50 = 30,000 positions before merging. A model that downsamples by 4 still sees 7,500, about as many as a 5,000-word document.
