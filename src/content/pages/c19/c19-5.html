Intuition

Some questions are about how things look: "a blue kurta with white embroidery", "the slide with the bar chart about churn". Words in the documents may not say it; the picture does. Multimodal embeddings put images and text in one space, so a text question can find a matching picture.

Most systems use both: text search catches exact names and numbers, image search catches appearance, and the two rankings are fused (9.5).

Mechanism

PatternHowUse when
Caption, then text searcha VLM writes a caption or description for each image; index the textsimple stacks; descriptions are useful to show users
Image embeddingsembed each image with a CLIP-style model; embed the query with its text encoderappearance matters: products, photos, diagrams
Page-image retrievalembed whole page images (late-interaction models such as ColPali)slides and PDFs whose layout and charts carry meaning
Hybrid with fusionrun text and image search, combine with reciprocal rank fusionmost production systems

Costs. Image embeddings are cheap to search but expensive to create at scale; captions add model cost at ingestion. Page images sent to the generator cost far more tokens than their text, so send only the top few.

Example: visual product search

A marketplace adds image search to its catalogue of 2 million listings. Text search alone found the right product in the top 3 for 61% of "looks like" queries; image embeddings alone 58%; fused, 74%. Exact model-number queries still rely on text search.

The numbers

Fusion by rank. A product is 2nd in the text ranking and 5th in the image ranking. With reciprocal rank fusion and the usual constant 60:

  • Text: 1 ÷ (60 + 2) ≈ 0.0161. Image: 1 ÷ (60 + 5) ≈ 0.0154. Fused score ≈ 0.0315.
  • A product 1st in text but 40th in image scores 1 ÷ 61 + 1 ÷ 100 ≈ 0.0164 + 0.0100 = 0.0264, so the first product, good on both, ranks higher.

Storage. 2 million images × 768 numbers × 4 bytes ≈ 6.1 GB of vectors before compression (9.4).
