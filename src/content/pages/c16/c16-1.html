Threat modelling asks, for each component and data flow: what can go wrong, who could cause it, and what controls prevent, detect or limit it. Draw the data-flow diagram, mark trust boundaries (where data crosses from less to more trusted zones), and walk each flow. Frameworks like STRIDE (spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial of service, elevation of privilege) structure the walk; the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications and MITRE ATLAS list AI-specific threats.

Example: one finding from a review

The assistant can read a user's email (private data), reads arbitrary inbound emails (untrusted content), and can render Markdown images (a channel to an external URL). Together these allow an attacker's email to instruct the model to embed private data in an image URL that the client fetches. This combination of private data + untrusted content + an exfiltration channel is sometimes called the "lethal trifecta"; remove at least one leg (here: block external image URLs in rendered output).
