Intuition

Before locking a house, you walk round it: which doors and windows are there, who might try them, and what stops them. Threat modelling is that walk for an AI system: users’ prompts, retrieved documents, tools, models and logs are all doors.

One combination deserves special care: an assistant that can see private data, reads content written by outsiders, and can send things out. With all three, a hidden instruction in an email can leak your data. Removing any one leg of this lethal trifecta breaks the attack.

Mechanism

Threat modelling asks, for each component and data flow: what can go wrong, who could cause it, and what controls prevent, detect or limit it. Draw the data-flow diagram, mark trust boundaries (where data crosses from less to more trusted zones), and walk each flow. Frameworks like STRIDE (spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial of service, elevation of privilege) structure the walk; the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications and MITRE ATLAS list AI-specific threats.

Example: one finding from a review

The assistant can read a user's email (private data), reads arbitrary inbound emails (untrusted content), and can render Markdown images (a channel to an external URL). Together these allow an attacker's email to instruct the model to embed private data in an image URL that the client fetches. This combination of private data + untrusted content + an exfiltration channel is sometimes called the "lethal trifecta"; remove at least one leg (here: block external image URLs in rendered output).

The numbers

Counting the review. An assistant with 6 components and 9 data flows gives 9 flows × 6 STRIDE categories = 54 questions to ask. Most have obvious answers; the few that do not are the findings.

Why combinations matter (illustrative). If 1 email in 10,000 carries an injection and the assistant processes 50,000 emails a day, about 5 attacks a day reach the model. With the trifecta in place, each one can try to leak data; with external links and images blocked, the same 5 attacks have no way out.

Code

The program checks five assistant components for private data, untrusted content and a way to send data out, flags the ones with all three, and shows that blocking one leg breaks the combination. Add your own component and run it again.
