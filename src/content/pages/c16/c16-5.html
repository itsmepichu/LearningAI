Intuition

AI systems copy data everywhere: into prompts, chunks, vectors, caches, memories, traces and training sets. Each copy is another place a leak can happen and another place a deletion request must reach.

So send the model only what it needs (data minimisation), encrypt and set a retention period for every copy, and keep an audit log of who saw what and what the system did, so you can answer questions later.

Mechanism

  • Classify data: public, internal, confidential, regulated (personal data, health, payment, export-controlled). Classification drives which models and regions may process it.
  • Minimise: send the model only what the task needs; redact or pseudonymise personal data (replace names with tokens and re-identify after) where possible.
  • Encryption: TLS in transit; encryption at rest for databases, indexes, object storage and backups; per-tenant keys (customer-managed keys) for high-sensitivity tenants, enabling crypto-shredding on exit.
  • Provider terms: confirm hosted-model contracts on data retention, training use, region and sub-processors; configure zero/limited retention where offered and required.
  • Retention and deletion: define retention per data type (prompts, outputs, traces, memories, eval datasets, fine-tuning sets). Deletion requests must reach every copy: source mirror, chunks, vectors, caches, memories, logs, evaluation and training datasets, and derived models (which may require retraining or per-tenant adapters).
  • Auditability: immutable logs of who accessed what, what the model was given, what actions it took and on whose behalf, which model/prompt versions were used, and approvals, retained per policy and queryable for investigations and regulators.
  • Derived data is still sensitive: embeddings can leak information about their source text, and summaries, memories and caches can repeat personal data verbatim. Classify, encrypt, retain and delete them under the same rules as the source.

The numbers

Copies of one document (illustrative). One policy file becomes 40 chunks, 40 vectors, perhaps 15 cached answers, 200 traces a month that quote it and 3 rows in an evaluation set. Deleting it means finding about 300 copies, which is why lineage (10.4) matters.

Retention adds up. 1 million traces a day at 20 KB each is 20 GB a day. Kept for 30 days: 600 GB of potentially personal data; kept for a year: about 7.3 TB.
