LLMs are strong at language transformation (summarise, rewrite, translate, extract), at pattern-following from a few examples, at writing and explaining code, and at broad general knowledge. Their weaknesses follow directly from "predict the likely next token from frozen weights":

  • Hallucination: fluent, confident output that is false. The model produces what is plausible; when it lacks the fact, a plausible-sounding fabrication is still the likely continuation. Invented citations, API methods and policy clauses are typical.
  • Outdated knowledge: weights reflect data up to a training cutoff. The model does not know about your product release last week, or your internal documents at all, unless you put them in the context (that is RAG, chapter 9).
  • Bias: training data reflects the world's skews. Models can associate professions with genders, rate dialects as less formal, or default to US conventions. In hiring, lending or moderation this becomes a legal and ethical problem.
  • Unreliable confidence: tone does not track correctness. "I'm certain" carries no information. Even token probabilities are only loosely related to factual accuracy after instruction tuning. Do not use the model's self-reported confidence as a control signal without measuring it (3.5, 8.3).
  • Other practical limits: arithmetic and exact counting errors (tokens are not characters), sensitivity to prompt wording, non-determinism, and vulnerability to instructions hidden in input text (prompt injection, chapter 16).
Example: a real failure pattern

An internal assistant is asked "Which config flag enables audit logging in our gateway?" It answers --enable-audit-log=true. That flag does not exist; the real one is audit.sink in a YAML file. The model generated the most typical shape of such a flag. Mitigation: retrieve the gateway's documentation, require answers to quote the doc, and add this question to the evaluation set.

In practice · HarborHarbor's test set includes a policy that never existed, a policy revised last week and a question asked in Tamil. The team grades whether each claim is supported, whether the cited version is current, and per-language failure rates, not just fluency.
