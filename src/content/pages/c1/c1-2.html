Intuition

Each era of AI fixed the biggest problem of the one before. First people wrote rules by hand, which broke on anything unexpected. Then computers learned weights from data, but people still had to choose what to look at. Then neural networks learned what to look at too. Then attention let a model look at a whole sentence at once instead of word by word, which made training fast on GPUs and opened the way to today's large models.

It is like the history of maps: hand-drawn sketches, then surveyed maps, then satellite photos, then live maps that update themselves. Each step kept what worked and removed one big limit.

Mechanism

Each era of AI solved the main limitation of the previous one, and introduced a new one. Knowing this chain tells you why transformers look the way they do.

Example: the same sentence across eras

Task: decide whether "The movie was not bad at all" is positive. A rule-based system that flags "bad" says negative. A bag-of-words classifier may also say negative because "bad" has a negative weight. An LSTM can learn that "not … at all" flips meaning. A transformer attends from "bad" to "not" directly, and a foundation model has seen millions of similar phrases during pretraining, so it gets it right with no task-specific training.

From 2012 to 2020 that is about 175,000,000,000 ÷ 60,000,000 ≈ 2,900 times bigger. Training text grew too: GPT-3 was trained on about 300 billion tokens, and Llama 3 (2024) on about 15 trillion, 50 times more.

Why transformers won. An RNN reads a 1,000-word text in 1,000 steps, one after another, because each step needs the one before. A transformer handles all 1,000 positions of a training text in one parallel pass, which is exactly the kind of work a GPU with thousands of cores is good at.
