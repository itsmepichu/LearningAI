Intuition

Pick the simplest tool that does the job. You would not hire a novelist to add up a bill, and you would not use a calculator to write a thank-you letter. Rules and code are the calculator: exact, free and easy to check. An LLM is the novelist: great with messy language, but slower, more expensive and sometimes wrong.

Most good systems mix them: code for anything that must be exact, search to find the facts, and an LLM only for the parts that really need language.

Mechanism

The most expensive mistake in enterprise AI is using an LLM where a simpler tool is more accurate, cheaper and easier to audit. Choose by asking what the task really is.

ApproachFits whenStrengthsWeaknesses
Rules / codeThe logic can be written down and must be exact: tax calculation, eligibility, validation, permissions.Deterministic, auditable, free to run.Brittle with messy language; costly to maintain if rules explode.
SearchThe answer exists in a document and users need to find it: policy lookup, product catalog.Shows sources; fast; no fabrication.Users must read and synthesise; poor with vague queries.
Traditional MLStructured or tabular data, a fixed set of outputs, plenty of labels: churn, fraud, demand forecasts.Cheap at scale, measurable, calibrated scores.Needs labelled data and feature work; does not generate text.
LLMOpen-ended language in or out: drafting, summarising, extracting from free text, conversational help, code.No training data to start, handles variety.Cost, latency, non-determinism, hallucination, injection risk.

Example: an insurance claims intake

"Is the policy active on the incident date?" is a database query and a date comparison: rules. "Which clause covers water damage?" is search. "Is this claim likely fraudulent?" is traditional ML on claim history. "Turn the customer's long email into the structured claim form" is an LLM job, with schema validation (7.4) and rules checking the extracted amounts. One product, four techniques.

The numbers

The same task, a million requests a month (illustrative prices). An LLM call with 1,500 tokens in and 200 out, at $1 and $4 per million tokens, costs 1,500 × 1 ÷ 1,000,000 + 200 × 4 ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.0023. A million calls is $2,300 a month, and each takes about a second and a half.

A rule that does the same job runs in about a millisecond and costs almost nothing. If rules can answer 70% of requests and only 30% go to the LLM, the LLM bill falls to 30% of $2,300 = $690, and 7 in 10 users get an instant, exact answer.

Accuracy is a cost too. If an LLM is right 97% of the time on a task a rule gets 100% right, then on a million requests that is 30,000 wrong answers a month to find, explain and fix.
