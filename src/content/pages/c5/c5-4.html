Intuition

The same transformer block can be wired three ways, and the wiring decides what the model is good at. A reader that sees the whole page at once is best at understanding it. A writer that can only see what it has written so far is built for producing text. A translator first reads a whole sentence with one part of itself and then writes with another, looking back at what it read.

The only difference between these three is who may look at whom: the attention mask.

Example: picking architectures in one RAG system

A bi-encoder (encoder-only) embeds documents and queries for fast vector search; a cross-encoder reranker (encoder-only, reading query and passage together) re-scores the top 50; a decoder-only LLM writes the answer. Three transformers, three architectures, each matched to its job.

The math

All three families use the same attention, \operatorname{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k} + M)V; they differ in the mask M and in where Q, K and V come from. Writing M_{ij} = 0 for "may attend" and -\infty for "may not":

M^{\text{enc}}_{ij} = 0 \ \ \forall\, i,j \qquad M^{\text{dec}}_{ij} = \begin{cases} 0 & j \le i \\ -\infty & j > i \end{cases}
M^{\text{cross}}_{ij} = 0,\ \ Q = H_{\text{dec}}W_Q,\ K = H_{\text{enc}}W_K,\ V = H_{\text{enc}}W_V

The training objectives follow from the masks. A decoder-only model maximises the probability of each next token given the ones before it; an encoder-only model predicts a random subset \mathcal{M} of masked tokens from everything else:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{causal}} = -\sum_{t=1}^{n} \log p(x_t \mid x_{<t}), \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{masked}} = -\sum_{t \in \mathcal{M}} \log p(x_t \mid x_{\setminus \mathcal{M}})

A causal mask allows n(n+1)/2 of the n^2 pairs. Because no token's representation depends on later tokens, the keys and values of earlier tokens never change during generation and can be cached (the KV cache); a bidirectional model would have to recompute them every time a token is added.
