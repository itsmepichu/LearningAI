Intuition

First impressions set expectations. If an assistant’s first screen promises it can do anything, every gap feels like a failure; if it shows what it is good at with examples, users ask the questions it can answer.

Failures will happen anyway. Graceful failure means each kind of failure has a designed response: a partial answer with a warning, a handover to a person, a saved request to retry, never a bare "Something went wrong".

Mechanism

MomentPatternExample
First usesay what it does and does not do; show example prompts"I can answer questions about your bookings and our fare rules"

Out of scopesay so and point to the right place"I can’t do that here; contact support"
Dependency failuresave the request, retry, tell the user"Saved; I’ll retry and notify you"
Slowoffer to wait or get the result later"Taking longer than usual: email it to me?"
Any failurea way to reach a persona visible human handover
Over-promising onboarding

"Ask me anything" invites questions the system cannot answer. Specific examples set honest expectations and lead users to the questions that work.

The numbers

Dead ends (illustrative). Of 1,000 conversations, 80% are answered; 7% end with low confidence, 5% out of scope, 4% with a tool failure and 4% with a timeout.

  • With one generic error message: 200 users hit a dead end.
  • With a designed next step for each and, say, half of those users recovering: about 100 more users get what they came for, with no model change at all.

Code

The program assigns simulated outcomes to your project’s messages and compares a generic error message with a designed message and next step for each kind of failure. Rewrite the messages in NEXT_STEP for your product.
