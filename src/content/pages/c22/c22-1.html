Intuition

AI is a good colleague for some jobs and a dangerous one for others. It shines where answers are hard to write as rules, mistakes are cheap, and a person can check the result quickly. It is a poor fit where rules already work, or where one mistake is expensive and hard to spot.

The design question is also how much to let it decide: the level of automation. Suggesting is safe almost anywhere; acting alone needs low error costs, cheap undo and evidence that it works.

Example: a refund feature

A team planned an assistant that approves refunds on its own. Mapping the task showed three parts: reading the customer’s message (a good fit for AI), calculating the amount (rules), and approving (high error cost). They shipped AI drafting with code computing the amount and a person approving refunds above a threshold (11.7).

The numbers

When review pays (illustrative). An agent writes a reply in 6 minutes; checking and editing an AI draft takes 2.

  • At 1,000 replies a day: 6,000 minutes by hand against 2,000 with drafts, a saving of 4,000 minutes, about 67 hours a day.
  • If drafts are wrong 10% of the time and a wrong draft costs 4 extra minutes: 100 × 4 = 400 minutes, still far less than the saving.

When acting alone does not. If each wrong refund costs 2,000 rupees to recover and 1% of 10,000 daily refunds are wrong: 100 × 2,000 = 200,000 rupees a day.
