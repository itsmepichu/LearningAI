Intuition

A lift with a floor display feels faster than one without, even when both take the same time. Perceived latency depends mostly on how soon users see that something is happening, and whether they can tell how far along it is.

So AI interfaces stream the answer as it is written, show the steps an agent has finished, and offer to send long results later, rather than leaving a spinner on screen for ten seconds.

Mechanism

WaitPatternWhy
Under 1 secondjust answerfeels instant
1–10 secondsstream tokens; show a short statusattention holds if something moves

Longerrun in the background; notify when donenobody watches a screen for minutes (15.4)

Streaming has costs. Text that later turns out wrong has already been read; validation that needs the whole answer (a JSON schema, a safety check) must run before showing it, or the interface must be able to replace streamed text. Stream prose; hold back structured actions until they are checked.

Example: an agent that explains its progress

A rebooking agent takes 20 seconds. Shown as a spinner, many users refreshed the page and started a second request. Shown as steps ("found your booking", "checked three alternative flights", "comparing fares"), almost nobody did, and duplicate requests fell sharply.

The numbers

First feedback decides (illustrative). The full answer takes 9 seconds; the first token comes at 0.8.

  • Waiting for the full answer: everyone sits 9 seconds with nothing on screen.
  • Streaming: something appears at 0.8 seconds, 9 ÷ 0.8 ≈ 11 times sooner, though the answer finishes at the same moment.
  • An agent with 6 steps of 4 seconds: 24 seconds in total, but showing each finished step means no silence longer than 4 seconds.
