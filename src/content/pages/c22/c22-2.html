Intuition

A good expert says "I’m not sure, but…" when they are not sure, and shows where they read something. An AI interface should do the same: show the sources behind an answer, mark statements it could not support, and say in plain words how far to trust it. That is showing uncertainty.

The danger runs both ways. Confident wrong answers teach users to trust too much; warnings on every answer teach them to ignore warnings.

Citations can mislead too

A citation to a real document that does not actually say what the sentence claims is worse than no citation, because it looks verified. Check support, not only the presence of a source id.

The numbers

Calibration before labels (illustrative). On 1,000 held-out answers, those the model scored above 0.85 were right 93% of the time; those between 0.6 and 0.85, 78%; below 0.6, 51%.

  • Labels "high", "medium" and "check this" then mean something users can learn.
  • If uncalibrated scores above 0.85 were right only 70% of the time, "high" would mislead 3 users in 10.

Too many warnings. If 40% of answers carry a warning, users see one every few answers and stop reading them. Aim the warning at the slice that needs it.

Code

The program builds an answer from two of your project’s documents plus one invented sentence, attaches a citation to each sentence by word overlap, flags the unsupported one, and maps scores to plain-language labels. Lower the overlap threshold and see the invented sentence get a citation it does not deserve.
