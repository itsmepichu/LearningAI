• Collection: web crawls (Common Crawl is the common starting point), code repositories, books, academic papers, encyclopedias, forums, licensed datasets, and synthetic data generated by other models.
  • Licensing and consent: copyright, terms of service, robots.txt preferences, personal data laws, and open-data licences constrain what may be used. This is an active legal area; enterprises adopting a model should read its data documentation and licence (16.6).
  • Filtering: language identification, removing boilerplate, spam and adult content, PII scrubbing, toxicity filtering, and quality classifiers (small models trained to score "educational" or "well-written" text). Datasets such as FineWeb-Edu showed quality filtering can significantly improve models at the same token count.
  • Deduplication: exact (hashes) and near-duplicate (MinHash + locality-sensitive hashing over word n-grams). Duplicates waste compute and increase memorisation.
  • Contamination: benchmark test questions leaking into training data, making scores meaningless. Detected by searching for n-gram overlaps with test sets; matters when you evaluate (8.6).
Example: a contamination check

Before claiming your fine-tuned model scores 92% on an internal QA benchmark, search the fine-tuning set for 13-gram overlaps with benchmark questions. Finding 40 of 500 questions verbatim in a scraped support forum used for training means those 40 must be removed from the benchmark or the training data, and the score recomputed.
