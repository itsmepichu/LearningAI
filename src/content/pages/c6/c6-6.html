Training memory per parameter with mixed-precision Adam is about 16 bytes: 2 (BF16 weights) + 2 (BF16 gradients) + 12 (FP32 master weights, Adam first and second moments). An 8B model needs ~128 GB before counting activations, more than any single GPU. Work is therefore split in several ways, usually combined ("3D parallelism"):

  • Data parallelism (DP): every GPU holds a full model copy and processes different batches; gradients are averaged with an all-reduce each step. Simple; limited by per-GPU memory.
  • Sharding (ZeRO / FSDP): data parallelism where optimiser states, gradients and even weights are split across GPUs and gathered just in time. Removes the memory redundancy of plain DP.
  • Tensor parallelism (TP): split individual weight matrices across GPUs (e.g. half the columns each). Needs very fast interconnect (NVLink), so used within a node.
  • Pipeline parallelism (PP): assign consecutive layers to different GPUs; micro-batches flow through like an assembly line. Creates idle "bubbles".
  • Also: sequence/context parallelism (split long sequences) and expert parallelism (place MoE experts on different GPUs).
Example: fitting a 70B fine-tune

Full fine-tuning 70B × 16 bytes ≈ 1.1 TB of state. On 8 × 80 GB GPUs (640 GB) it does not fit even with full sharding, before activations. Options: more nodes with FSDP, CPU offloading (slow), or parameter-efficient fine-tuning, where only small adapter matrices are trained (13.3). QLoRA can fine-tune a 70B model on a single 80 GB GPU.
