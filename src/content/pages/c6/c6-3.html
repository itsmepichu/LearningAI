Intuition

A training run is a budget problem. You have a fixed amount of computer time, and you can spend it on a bigger model or on more reading. Scaling laws are the price list: from small test runs, they predict how good a big run will be, so nobody has to gamble millions on a guess.

The data mixture is the diet: how much web, code, books and maths the model reads. Change the diet and you change what it is good at, even with the same budget.

Mechanism

A data mixture is the proportion of each source in training (e.g. 60% web, 15% code, 10% books and papers, 8% multilingual, 7% maths). Mixtures are tuned with small-scale experiments because they strongly shape capabilities. Often a higher-quality slice is upweighted near the end of training ("annealing").

Scaling laws are empirical relationships: loss falls smoothly and predictably as a power law in parameters N, tokens D and compute C. Two results are worth memorising:

  • Training compute ≈ 6 · N · D FLOPs (2 for the forward pass, 4 for backward, per parameter per token).
  • Chinchilla-optimal (2022): for a fixed compute budget, loss is minimised with roughly 20 tokens per parameter. Modern models are deliberately trained far past this (hundreds or thousands of tokens per parameter) because a smaller model trained longer is cheaper to serve, and serving costs dominate over a model's lifetime.
Example: an 8B model on 15T tokens

C = 6 × 8×10⁹ × 15×10¹² = 7.2×10²³ FLOPs. At 40% MFU (≈ 4×10¹⁴ FLOP/s per GPU) that is ≈ 1.8×10⁹ GPU-seconds ≈ 500,000 GPU-hours, the same order of magnitude as published figures for models of this size. At an illustrative $2.50 per GPU-hour, ≈ $1.25M for the final run alone. This is why enterprises adapt existing models rather than pretrain.

The numbers

Compute-optimal against train-longer. For an 8-billion-parameter model, the compute-optimal point is about 20 tokens per parameter: 20 × 8 billion = 160 billion tokens. Llama 3 8B was trained on about 15 trillion, roughly 1,900 tokens per parameter, about 94 times more. That costs more to train but gives a small model that is cheaper every time it answers.

What a run costs. 8B parameters on 15 trillion tokens is about 500,000 GPU-hours at 40% utilisation. On 1,024 GPUs that is 500,000 ÷ 1,024 ≈ 490 hours, about 20 days, and at an illustrative $2.50 per GPU-hour about $1.25 million, for the final run alone.

A mixture in tokens. If 15% of 15 trillion tokens are code, the model reads 2.25 trillion tokens of code.

Primary sources

  2. Hoffmann et al. (2022), Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Chinchilla) The 20-tokens-per-parameter result.
  3. Chowdhery et al. (2022), PaLM: Scaling Language Modeling with Pathways Defines model FLOPs utilisation (MFU) and reports it for a large run.
