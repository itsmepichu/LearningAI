Intuition

Making an assistant happens in stages, like training a doctor. First comes years of general reading (pretraining): the model reads a huge slice of the internet and learns language, facts and patterns, but it only knows how to continue text. Then comes residency (supervised fine-tuning): it studies thousands of example conversations and learns to answer like an assistant. Last comes feedback from supervisors (preference tuning): it learns which of its own answers people prefer.

The first stage takes almost all the effort. Everything after it is small by comparison, which is why companies can adapt an existing model instead of building one.

Mechanism

Pretraining: next-token prediction on a huge general corpus. Produces a base model that continues text but does not reliably follow instructions. This stage uses >95% of total compute.
Continued pretraining (domain-adaptive pretraining): more next-token training on domain text (legal, medical, code, a new language) to shift knowledge and vocabulary use.
Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) / instruction tuning: train on curated (instruction, response) conversations so the model learns to act as an assistant.
Preference optimisation: train on comparisons ("response A is better than B") via RLHF or DPO, and increasingly on verifiable rewards (tests pass, maths answer correct) to improve helpfulness, safety and reasoning.
Example: base vs instruct

Prompt "Write a haiku about Kubernetes". A base model may continue: "Write a haiku about Docker. Write a haiku about…" because lists of prompts are common online. The instruct model writes the haiku. Fine-tuning for a narrow format usually starts from the instruct model; continued pretraining usually starts from the base model.

The numbers

How lopsided the stages are for an 8-billion-parameter model (illustrative token counts):

StageTraining tokens
Pretraining15 trillion
Supervised fine-tuning: 100,000 conversations × 600 tokens60 million
Preference tuning: 50,000 pairsa few hundred million, counting the extra passes

15 trillion ÷ 60 million = 250,000: pretraining reads a quarter of a million times more text than fine-tuning. At about 500,000 GPU-hours, pretraining costs millions of dollars; fine-tuning the same model can take a few GPU-hours.
