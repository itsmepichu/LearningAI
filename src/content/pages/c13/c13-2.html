Fine-tuning learns whatever the data shows, including its mistakes and inconsistencies. A few thousand consistent examples beat tens of thousands of noisy ones.

  • Source inputs from real production traffic (privacy-reviewed, PII handled per 16.5), covering the real distribution plus hard cases.
  • Write a labelling guideline: task definition, output schema, decision rules, many worked examples, explicit edge cases, and what to do when unsure. Version it.
  • Produce outputs: experts write them, or a strong model drafts them and experts correct (much faster; watch for annotators rubber-stamping).
  • Quality checks: double-label a sample and measure agreement; expert audit of random samples per annotator; automatic checks (schema validity, length, banned content); deduplicate; check for contamination with the evaluation set (6.2).
  • Split: hold out a test set first (by customer or time if leakage is possible), never trained on.
Example: QC finding

Spot-checking 200 records shows one annotator accepted 99% of model drafts unchanged while others edited 22%. Their accepted records contain three times the error rate. Their batch is re-reviewed and the review tool changes to hide the model draft for 10% of items (a blind check).

In practice · HarborHarbor's extraction guideline says a missing effective date is null, never inferred; two annotators label a shared sample and resolve disagreements before scaling up.
