Intuition

When a model falls short, there is a ladder of fixes. Better instructions cost an afternoon. Adding a search step costs days. Fine-tuning costs weeks and a dataset. Teaching a small model to copy a big one (distillation) or continuing pretraining costs more again, and each rung brings its own upkeep.

So climb one rung at a time, and only when measurement shows the current rung cannot meet the target. The symptom tells you which rung: missing facts point to retrieval, wrong behaviour despite good examples points to fine-tuning, and a good but expensive model points to distillation.

Mechanism

Climb this ladder one rung at a time, and move up only when evaluation shows the current rung cannot reach the acceptance criteria.

Symptom found in evaluationLikely remedy
Wrong or outdated facts; no citationsRetrieval
Right content, wrong format or style, despite examplesFine-tune (SFT)
Large model accurate but cost/latency too high at volumeDistillation to a small model
Prompt needs 4,000 tokens of rules on every callFine-tune the rules in; keep a short prompt
Model misunderstands domain vocabulary everywhereContinued pretraining (or a domain model) + SFT
Retrieval misses domain synonyms and codesFine-tune the embedding model or reranker (13.5)
Example: a distillation business case (illustrative)

A logistics firm classifies 3M shipping exceptions per month. A large hosted model reaches 94% at $0.004 each ($12,000/month). Distilling into a fine-tuned 3B open model on 40,000 teacher-labelled and human-spot-checked examples reaches 93% at an estimated $0.0003 each on a self-hosted GPU (about $900/month) with lower latency. The 1-point drop was accepted after checking it was not concentrated on critical exception types.

The numbers

The distillation example, worked through (illustrative).

  • Large hosted model: 3,000,000 items × 0.004 dollars = 12,000 dollars a month.
  • Small self-hosted model: about 900 dollars a month for the GPU. Add an engineer’s share for upkeep, say 1,500 dollars: 2,400 dollars a month.
  • Saving: 12,000 − 2,400 = 9,600 dollars a month. A one-off cost of 6,000 dollars for labelling and training is paid back in about 6,000 ÷ 9,600 ≈ 0.6 months.

At lower volume it fails. At 300,000 items the large model costs 1,200 dollars a month, less than the 2,400 the small one needs, so distillation never pays.

Code

The program compares the large and the distilled model at five monthly volumes, including upkeep, and works out the payback time and the break-even volume. Change UPKEEP_MONTH or LARGE_PER_ITEM and run it again.
