Intuition

A general search model has never seen your company’s codenames, part numbers or abbreviations, so it matches them badly. Fine-tuning the search model on your own question–passage pairs teaches it what your words mean, and it is far cheaper than fine-tuning an LLM.

The most useful training examples are near misses: a passage that shares words with the question but does not answer it. These hard negatives teach the fine distinctions that easy examples never do.

Mechanism

General embedding models often miss domain meaning: internal product codenames, part numbers, legal terms of art, abbreviations. Fine-tuning the retriever is often the highest-return adaptation in a RAG system, and it is much cheaper than fine-tuning an LLM.

  • Training data: (query, relevant passage) pairs from search logs (queries with clicked/used results), from FAQ structures, from human labelling, or generated synthetically by an LLM writing questions for each chunk (then filtered).
  • Hard negatives: passages that look relevant but are not, mined by retrieving top results with the current model and excluding true positives. They teach fine distinctions and matter more than more easy pairs.
  • Loss: contrastive with in-batch negatives (MultipleNegativesRankingLoss / InfoNCE, 4.4). Rerankers (cross-encoders) train as binary or listwise classifiers over (query, passage).
  • Re-embedding: a new embedding model means a full re-index with blue/green migration (10.7). A new reranker does not touch the index, which makes rerankers the easier first step.

The numbers

Building the pairs (illustrative). 20,000 chunks × 2 generated questions = 40,000 candidate pairs. If a filter drops the 15% the chunk does not really answer, 34,000 remain.

Mining negatives. For each question, retrieve the top 20 with the current model and keep up to 2 that are not the source: about 68,000 hard negatives.

Measuring. On 300 real labelled queries, Recall@10 rising from 0.71 to 0.83 means 0.83 × 300 − 0.71 × 300 = 249 − 213 = 36 more queries find their passage in the top ten.

Code

The program uses each of your project’s document titles as a query, ranks the documents with a keyword scorer, and keeps the high-ranked wrong ones as hard negatives for contrastive training. Use the document text’s first sentence as the query instead and run it again.

Laptop lab: fine-tune a small embedding model Laptop · CPU · minutes for ~1k pairs
from datasets import Dataset
from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer, SentenceTransformerTrainer, losses
model = SentenceTransformer("BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5")
train = Dataset.from_dict({
    "anchor":   ["how do I raise a PO in Coupa", "SKU-77 torque spec", "what is Project Falcon"],
    "positive": ["Purchase orders are created in Coupa under Requisitions > New...",
                 "SKU-77 mounting bolts: tighten to 45 Nm in a star pattern...",
                 "Falcon is the internal codename for the 2027 billing platform rewrite..."]})
SentenceTransformerTrainer(model=model, train_dataset=train,
                           loss=losses.MultipleNegativesRankingLoss(model)).train()
model.save_pretrained("bge-small-acme")

With a few hundred real pairs you can measure Recall@k before and after using your chapter 9 harness. Expect gains on jargon; watch for losses on general queries (13.6).
