Intuition

Each kind of task needs its own kind of example. An extractor learns from documents paired with the fields they contain, and also with fields they do not contain. Show it only filled-in forms and it learns that every form is filled in, so it makes values up: field fabrication.

The same holds for tool use: include cases where no tool is needed, or the model learns that every question needs one. And before training a large model to classify, try a small one; it is often as good and far cheaper.

Example: an extraction regression caught

A fine-tuned contract extractor reaches 97% field accuracy, but on contracts with no termination clause it invents one 31% of the time, because only 2% of training examples had the field absent. Adding 600 absent-field examples brings fabrication to 2% with no loss elsewhere.

The numbers

Why the overall score hides it (the contract example). Suppose 1,000 test contracts, 900 with a termination clause and 100 without.

  • On the 900, the extractor is right 97% of the time: 873 right.
  • On the 100 without, it invents a clause 31% of the time: 69 right, 31 wrong.
  • Overall: (873 + 69) ÷ 1,000 = 94.2%, which looks fine, while nearly a third of the absent cases are fabricated.

Report the absent-field rate on its own line. In the example, adding 600 absent-field examples brought it from 31% to 2%.

Code

The program builds a one-field extraction set from the sentences of your project’s documents, removes the number from every other sentence, and scores a simulated extractor trained with 2%, 10% and 30% absent examples. Change TRIALS or the fabrication rule and run it again.
