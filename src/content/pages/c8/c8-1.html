Evaluation starts with writing down what "correct" means. A task definition states inputs, expected outputs and what counts as a good output. Acceptance criteria are the measurable thresholds for shipping ("≥ 92% correct category, 0 urgent tickets mislabelled non-urgent in the critical set, p95 latency ≤ 2 s, ≤ $0.002 per ticket").

A representative evaluation dataset mirrors production: sampled from real traffic (with privacy controls), stratified so important segments are present (languages, customer tiers, document types), including the hard and rare cases that matter most, labelled using a written guideline. Keep it versioned and separate from any data used to write prompts or fine-tune (to avoid overfitting your own test).

A baseline is what you compare against: the current production system, a simple rule, or "no AI" (human-only). A result without a baseline cannot be interpreted.

Example: acceptance criteria for a contract-clause extractor

Field-level exact match ≥ 95% for dates and amounts; ≥ 90% for party names after normalisation; 100% abstention ("not found") when the clause is absent in the 30 absent-clause cases; zero fabricated clause text in the 50-case critical set; p95 ≤ 8 s per contract; cost ≤ $0.05 per contract. Baseline: the paralegal team's 2-minute manual process with 97% accuracy.
