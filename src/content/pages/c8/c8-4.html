Intuition

One score cannot describe an answer. A reply can be correct but invent a source, grounded but leave out half of what was asked, or perfectly safe because it refuses almost everything. And a great answer that takes 20 seconds or costs a dollar may still be the wrong choice.

So measure several things side by side, like a car review that scores speed, comfort, safety and running cost separately instead of giving one number.

Mechanism

DimensionQuestion it answersHow to measure
Accuracy / correctnessIs the answer right?Exact match or tolerance vs reference; judge with reference answer; expert review
Groundedness / faithfulnessIs every claim supported by the provided sources?Claim-level judge against retrieved context (8.2); citation validity checks
CompletenessDid it cover all required points?Checklist of required facts per case; recall of key points
Refusal behaviourDoes it decline when it should, and only then?Should-refuse set (unsafe, out of scope, no answer in sources) and should-answer set; measure both over-refusal and under-refusal
LatencyIs it fast enough?Time to first token, total time; p50, p95, p99 (17.3)
CostIs it affordable at scale?Tokens in/out × price, plus retrieval and tool costs; per task and per successful task (15.9)
Example: over-refusal is also a failure

After adding "only answer from the provided documents", groundedness rises to 98% but the assistant now refuses 22% of answerable questions because relevant information was phrased differently. Track "should-answer refusal rate" next to groundedness so you see both sides of the tradeoff.

Refusing answerable questions is called over-refusal; measure it next to groundedness, or a stricter prompt can look like an improvement when it is not.

The numbers

Groundedness claim by claim. An answer with 5 claims, 4 of them supported by the sources, scores 4 ÷ 5 = 80% supported, and fails a strict "every claim supported" check.

Over-refusal in numbers. In the example below, 22% of answerable questions are refused. Out of 1,000 answerable questions a day, that is 220 customers turned away to make the groundedness score look better.

Cost per successful task. A call costing $0.02 that succeeds 80% of the time costs $0.02 ÷ 0.8 = $0.025 per success. A cheaper call at $0.01 with 50% success costs $0.02 per success, barely cheaper, and leaves twice as many failures.

Code

The program takes sentences from your example project’s documents as answers, makes a version with a changed number and one with an added claim, and checks every claim against the source; then it works out over- and under-refusal from example counts. Change the 0.7 overlap threshold and run it again.
