DimensionQuestion it answersHow to measure
Accuracy / correctnessIs the answer right?Exact match or tolerance vs reference; judge with reference answer; expert review
Groundedness / faithfulnessIs every claim supported by the provided sources?Claim-level judge against retrieved context (8.2); citation validity checks
CompletenessDid it cover all required points?Checklist of required facts per case; recall of key points
Refusal behaviourDoes it decline when it should, and only then?Should-refuse set (unsafe, out of scope, no answer in sources) and should-answer set; measure both over-refusal and under-refusal
LatencyIs it fast enough?Time to first token, total time; p50, p95, p99 (17.3)
CostIs it affordable at scale?Tokens in/out × price, plus retrieval and tool costs; per task and per successful task (15.9)
Example: over-refusal is also a failure

After adding "only answer from the provided documents", groundedness rises to 98% but the assistant now refuses 22% of answerable questions because relevant information was phrased differently. Track "should-answer refusal rate" next to groundedness so you see both sides of the tradeoff.

In practice · HarborHarbor's scorecard tracks correctness, groundedness, completeness (exceptions included), justified abstention, p95 latency and cost per accepted answer side by side.
