Intuition

An offline test tells you whether the new version answers your saved questions better. Only real users tell you whether it actually helps: fewer escalations, faster resolutions, more sales. An A/B test is a fair coin toss for each user, half get the old version and half the new, so any difference in outcomes is down to the change.

The catch is patience. Small improvements need many users to see clearly, and peeking at the results every day and stopping as soon as they look good will fool you.

Mechanism

Offline experiments run candidates on stored datasets: fast, safe, repeatable, but limited to what the dataset represents. Online experiments expose real users. In an A/B test, users are randomly assigned to control (A) or treatment (B) and you compare outcomes; randomisation makes the difference attributable to the change. Decide the primary metric, guardrail metrics (latency, complaints, cost) and sample size before starting, and do not stop early when the result looks good ("peeking" inflates false positives unless you use a sequential testing method).

Business outcomes are what the organisation actually cares about: tickets resolved without escalation, time saved per analyst, conversion, retention, error rates in downstream processes. Model metrics are leading indicators; business metrics are the goal, and often arrive with delay (17.7).

Example

Illustrative numbers. Offline, new prompt B improves answer quality by 4 points. In the A/B test, B's self-resolution rate is +1.1 points (95% CI +0.3 to +1.9), but average conversation length grew 18% and cost per conversation rose 12%. The business review decides the gain is worth it for premium customers only.

The chance that a test detects a real effect of a given size is its power; 80% is the usual target.

The numbers

How many users. With a 30% self-resolution rate today, detecting a 2-point lift (to 32%) with 80% power needs about 8,400 users in each group. At 4,000 eligible users a day split in two, that is 8,400 ÷ 2,000 ≈ 4 to 5 days.

Halve the lift, quadruple the users. To detect a 1-point lift instead, you need roughly four times as many users, about 33,000 per group, which takes over two weeks at the same traffic.

The peeking trap. With no real difference at all, checking the result every day for 20 days and stopping at the first "significant" day declares a winner in about a quarter of tests, five times the 5% you think you are accepting.
