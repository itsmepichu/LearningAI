Intuition

A general practitioner knows a little about everything; a specialist clinic that only does eye tests can be faster and cheaper for that one job. A small language model is the clinic: weak at open-ended questions, but on one narrow task with good training data it can match a model a hundred times its size.

The question is never "is the small model as smart?", but "is it good enough on this task, measured on our own data, at the volume we run?"

Hidden costs. A small model must be trained, served, monitored and retrained when inputs drift (18.6); a hosted large model needs none of that. At low volume, the large model is usually cheaper overall (13.1).

Example: ticket routing

A support team routes 1 million messages a month into 12 queues. A large model reaches 95% at 0.004 dollars a message, 4,000 dollars a month. A 300-million-parameter classifier fine-tuned on 20,000 labelled messages reaches 94% at a fraction of a cent per thousand and answers in 20 ms. The 1-point gap sits in one rare queue, which is routed to the large model.

The numbers

The routing example (illustrative).

  • Large model: 1,000,000 × 0.004 = 4,000 dollars a month.
  • Small model on a shared CPU server: about 20 dollars a month for compute, plus upkeep.
  • Accuracy: 95% against 94% means 10,000 more misrouted messages a month. If a misroute costs 2 minutes of an agent’s time, that is 20,000 minutes, about 330 hours: compare that with the 3,980 dollars saved.
