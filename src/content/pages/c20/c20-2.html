Intuition

Distillation starts with a teacher: a large model that labels or answers thousands of real inputs, so a small student can learn from them. The teacher is not always right, and the student copies its mistakes as faithfully as its successes.

So the real work is choosing good inputs and throwing away bad outputs: keep what the teacher is sure of, what it says consistently, what passes checks, and what humans spot-check, and test on labels written by people, never by the teacher.

Licences

Some providers’ terms restrict using their outputs to train competing models. Check the teacher’s terms and record them with the dataset (16.6).

The numbers

Filtering (illustrative). The teacher labels 10,000 inputs and is wrong on 12%: 1,200 bad labels.

  • Keeping only cases where two samples agree keeps, say, 7,700 with about 1% wrong: 77 bad labels.
  • Adding a confidence threshold keeps 5,000 with almost none wrong, but rare labels may disappear: check the label counts after filtering.

Cost. Two teacher calls per input at 0.004 dollars: 10,000 × 2 × 0.004 = 80 dollars, small next to the review time.
