Intuition

A teacher marking a test can say just "wrong", or can say "you picked B; A was right, and C was almost right too". The second tells the student much more. Soft targets are that richer answer: the teacher’s whole spread of probabilities, not only its top choice.

When you cannot see a hosted teacher’s probabilities, you train on its generated outputs instead, sometimes including its step-by-step explanations. That is sequence-level distillation, and it is how most distillation from API models works.

Mechanism

Temperature. Dividing the teacher’s scores by a temperature above 1 before the softmax flattens the distribution, so the student sees which wrong answers are "nearly right". The student is trained to match these soft probabilities, usually mixed with the true labels.

Example: distilling a classifier

A team distils a 7B teacher into a 300M student for intent detection. With hard labels the student reaches 91%; adding soft targets at temperature 2 reaches 93% with the same 20,000 examples, because the teacher’s probabilities show which intents are easily confused.

The numbers

Softening. Teacher scores 6.0, 3.5, 0.5 and −1.0.

  • At temperature 1 the probabilities are about 0.92, 0.08, 0.004 and 0.001: almost a hard label.
  • At temperature 4, the scores become 1.5, 0.875, 0.125 and −0.25, and the probabilities about 0.51, 0.27, 0.13 and 0.09: the student now sees that the second answer is a near miss.

Data needed. Soft targets give more information per example, so a student often reaches the same accuracy with fewer examples, which matters when teacher calls are expensive.

Code
