Intuition

A hospital does not send every patient to a consultant. A nurse handles most cases and refers the difficult ones. A model cascade works the same way: a small, cheap model answers first, and only the requests it is unsure about, or that fail a check, go to a large model.

The art is in the referral rule. Refer too little and quality drops; refer too much and you pay for the large model anyway.

Calibrate before you trust confidence. A model that says 90% should be right about 90% of the time. Measure it on held-out data, and choose the threshold from a curve of accuracy against the share sent to the large model.

Example: a cascade for support routing

A small classifier answers 85% of messages with confidence above 0.9 and is 98% right on those; the other 15% go to a large model. Overall accuracy is 96%, close to the large model alone, at about a sixth of its cost.

The numbers

Cost and accuracy (the example, illustrative). Small model 0.00002 dollars, large 0.004.

  • Every request pays the small model: 0.00002. 15% also pay the large model: 0.15 × 0.004 = 0.0006. Total about 0.00062 per request, against 0.004 for the large model alone, about a sixth.
  • Accuracy: 0.85 × 98% + 0.15 × 85% (hard cases are harder for everyone) ≈ 83.3% + 12.8% ≈ 96%.

Raising the threshold so 30% go to the large model doubles the large-model cost; it is worth it only if accuracy rises enough.

Code

The program trains a small classifier on 30 of your project’s examples, sends low-confidence cases to an assumed large model, and shows the share escalated, accuracy and cost for several thresholds. Change LARGE_COST or the training size and run it again.
