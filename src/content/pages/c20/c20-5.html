Intuition

Running a model on the device is like carrying a pocket dictionary instead of phoning a library: always available, private and free per use, but smaller and slower for hard questions.

The limits are physical: how much memory the device can spare, how fast it can read that memory, how hot it gets and how much battery it uses. A model has to fit all four.

Why bother. Privacy (data stays on the device), offline use, no per-request cost, and low latency for short tasks. Costs: shipping updates to millions of devices, many hardware variants to test, and no central place to log failures, so evaluation has to happen before release.

Example: offline itinerary help

A travel app ships a 1-billion-parameter model in 4-bit (about 0.6 GB) to answer questions about a downloaded itinerary offline. It handles "what time is my train?" locally; anything needing live data goes to the server when the phone is online.

The numbers

Fit and speed on a laptop CPU with 16 GB and about 80 GB/s.

  • A 3B model in 4-bit: 3 × 0.55 ≈ 1.7 GB. Decode reads it all per token: 80 ÷ 1.7 ≈ 48 tokens a second at best.
  • An 8B model in 16-bit: 16 GB, more than half the machine’s memory, so it does not fit comfortably.
  • A phone with 8 GB and about 50 GB/s running the 3B 4-bit model: at most 50 ÷ 1.7 ≈ 30 tokens a second, less when it heats up.
