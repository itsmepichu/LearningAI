Intuition

People have a right to know when they are dealing with a machine, and others need to know when content was made by one. AI disclosure and labelling covers both: a clear notice in conversations, and marks on generated content, visible to people and readable by software.

Behind the scenes, record-keeping makes the system answerable later: who did what, with which model and version, and who approved it.

Example: decision records that survive an audit

An auditor asks why a claim was rejected eight months ago. The record shows the input reference, the model and prompt versions, the draft the system produced, the reviewer who edited it and the final decision. Without the versions, the team could not have reproduced the draft.

Orientation for engineers, not legal advice. Laws, guidance and application dates change; confirm what applies with your legal and compliance team.

The numbers

Completeness (illustrative). 500 decision records, each with 8 required fields. If any one field goes missing 2% of the time, the chance a record is complete is 0.98 multiplied 8 times ≈ 85%.

  • That is about 75 incomplete records in 500, each a hole in the audit trail.
  • A completeness check in the pipeline turns that into an alert on the first missing field instead of a finding a year later.

Storage. 1 million decisions a year at 2 KB each is 2 GB a year: record-keeping is cheap; missing records are not.

Code

The program checks 500 simulated decision records for eight required fields, reports which are missing, and labels a generated text with a visible notice and attached metadata. Add a required field and see completeness fall.
