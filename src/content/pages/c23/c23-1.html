Intuition

A law says what must be true; it rarely says how. "Keep a person in control of consequential decisions" does not name a screen or a log table. Engineering turns each obligation into a control, something the system does, and into evidence, the record that proves it was done.

The result is a table anyone can audit: obligation, control, evidence, owner and date. Gaps in that table are the real compliance risk.

Evidence made by hand goes stale

A screenshot taken for last year’s audit proves nothing about today. Prefer evidence the pipeline produces on every release or every decision, and date it.

Orientation for engineers, not legal advice. Laws, guidance and application dates change; confirm what applies with your legal and compliance team.

The numbers

Size of the job (illustrative). 25 obligations, 2 controls each on average, 1 piece of evidence per control: 50 rows to keep current.

  • If evidence is refreshed by hand every 6 months at 1 hour a row, that is 50 × 2 = 100 hours a year, and much of it is stale at any moment.
  • If 40 of the 50 are produced automatically by the pipeline, the manual work drops to 10 × 2 = 20 hours.

Staleness. With a 180-day freshness rule, evidence dated 250 days ago fails, even if the control still works: the drill must be run again.

Code

The program maps five example obligations to controls and each control to its latest evidence, and flags missing controls, missing evidence and evidence older than 180 days. Add a control for the deletion obligation and run it again.
