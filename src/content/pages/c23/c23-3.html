Intuition

Privacy laws give people rights over their data: to see what an organisation holds about them, to correct it and, in many cases, to have it deleted. Those rights were written with databases in mind; AI systems scatter copies into chunks, vectors, caches, memories, logs, evaluation sets and even model weights.

So privacy has to be designed in: know where every copy lives (lineage, 10.4), keep as little as possible, and build deletion that reaches every store, with proof.

Transfers and processorsknow where providers process data; contracts and region controls
Models remember

Deleting training rows does not remove what a model learned from them. Keep personal data out of training sets where possible, use per-tenant adapters that can be dropped, and flag models trained on deleted data for review (13.6).

Orientation for engineers, not legal advice. Laws, guidance and application dates change; confirm what applies with your legal and compliance team.

The numbers

Copies of one person’s data (illustrative): 3 source records, 12 chunks and vectors, 4 cached answers, 2 memories, 40 traces, 1 evaluation case, 2 fine-tuning rows and 3 backups: 67 copies in 8 stores.

  • Deleting the source alone removes 3 of 67, under 5%.
  • If traces are kept 30 days and the deadline to respond is a month, waiting for traces to expire on their own is not a plan: delete or redact them.

Code

The program finds a fictional customer’s data in eight stores, deletes what can be deleted, records what is retained and why, and notes what deleting training rows cannot undo. Add a store your own system has and run it again.
