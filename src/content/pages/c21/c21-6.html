Intuition

A season ticket is cheaper per match than paying at the gate, but only if you go to enough matches. Committed spend works the same way: promise a provider a fixed amount of usage, or reserve GPUs for a year, and pay a lower rate whether you use it or not.

Two things make it tricky for AI. Usage is hard to forecast, and prices keep falling as new models arrive. Commit too much and you pay for unused capacity; commit for too long and you are locked into last year’s price.

Negotiating. Ask for model-swap rights (the commitment moves to newer models), price-drop protection, the ability to shift spend between regions, and data-handling terms in the same contract (16.5). Plan for deprecations (18.5): a commitment tied to a model retired in six months is a liability.

The numbers

Is the commitment worth it? On-demand costs 1.00 a unit; committed units cost 0.70 each, paid even if unused. Forecast: 100 units a month, give or take 25.

  • Commit 100: in a month that uses 75 units you pay 100 × 0.70 = 70, still less than 75 on demand, though 25 paid units sat idle.
  • A month with 60 units used: commitment 70 against 60 on demand, a loss of 10.
  • Committing a bit below the forecast, about 85, keeps most of the discount and rarely pays for idle units.

Price cuts. If on-demand prices fall 40% halfway through the year, on demand becomes 0.60, below the committed 0.70: a long commitment then costs more than not committing.
