Intuition

Next-day delivery costs less than delivery within the hour. Model providers work the same way: batch processing accepts many requests at once, returns them within a day, and charges much less, because the provider can run them when its machines are quiet.

A lot of AI work has no one waiting: nightly summaries, re-embedding, evaluation runs, report drafts. Moving that work to batch, or to off-peak hours on your own GPUs, is often the easiest saving there is.

Off-peak on your own GPUs. Self-hosted capacity is sized for the daytime peak and idle at night (14.8). Scheduling bulk work into those hours costs almost nothing extra and raises average utilisation.

Example: re-embedding at night

A team re-embeds 2 million changed chunks each month. On the hosted batch interface at half price it costs 2,000 dollars instead of 4,000; on their own GPUs between 01:00 and 06:00 it costs nothing extra, because those GPUs would otherwise sit idle.

The numbers

Which jobs fit (illustrative, 4 dollars per million tokens, batch at half price, 24-hour window).

  • Re-embedding: 2,000,000 × 500 tokens = 1 billion tokens: 4,000 dollars, 2,000 in batch.
  • Evaluation runs: 80,000 × 6,000 = 480 million tokens: 1,920 dollars, 960 in batch.
  • Nightly summaries due in 12 hours do not fit a 24-hour window; they stay real-time or run on idle GPUs.

Live chat dominates the bill and cannot move, so batch saves a modest share overall but is nearly free to adopt.
