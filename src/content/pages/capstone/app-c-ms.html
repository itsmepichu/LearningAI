Intuition

The capstone is a small but complete version of your example project, built in eight milestones. Each milestone is like a driving-test manoeuvre: a specific thing to build, the evidence to keep, and a pass condition written before you start, so "done" is a measurement and not a feeling.

Keep the data and traffic modest. What makes it convincing is not size but honesty: measured results next to an explicit design for scale, with each kept apart from the other.

Mechanism

Build a lite version of your example project (Harbor by default, or the online store, bank or travel agent; switch with the Example picker): a small but complete system. Keep data and traffic modest, and write an explicit scale design next to your measured results, so your reasoning about enterprise operation is concrete without pretending a laptop test is production.

The numbers

Reading milestone 3. On 100 labelled questions, Recall@10 rising from 0.71 to 0.88 means 0.88 × 100 − 0.71 × 100 = 88 − 71 = 17 more questions find their answer in the top ten.

  • Compare the two systems question by question: if the new one wins 21 and loses 4, the net gain is 17.
  • Resample the 100 questions 2,000 times and recompute the gain each time; if 95% of those gains are above zero, keep the component. If not, write down why you did not add it.

Milestone 4. pass^5 means a task passes all 5 repeated runs: a task that passes each run 90% of the time passes all five only 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9 ≈ 59% of the time.

Code

The program builds labelled questions from your project’s documents, compares a baseline retriever with a candidate question by question, and decides with a paired bootstrap interval whether the candidate stays. Change K to 3 and run it again.
